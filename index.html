The Malaysian Food Security Context

Under Malaysia's National Agrofood Policy 2.0 (DAN 2.0), aquaculture is marked as a critical high-growth sector to meet the target Self-Sufficiency Level (SSL) for fish proteins. Feed costs account for up to 70% of total production costs in Red Tilapia cage farming.

A central challenge in bioscience resource planning is modeling growth velocity. Farmers cannot easily determine when growth rate peaks by looking at cumulative weight graphs. By using the first derivative \( f'(x) \) of the logistic biomass curve, bioengineers can locate the exact Inflection Point—the precise moment of maximum growth velocity.

By matching feeding regimes to peak metabolic growth windows, feed waste is minimized, pollution in aquatic ecosystems is mitigated, and harvest windows are scientifically optimized.

Kinetics Equations

Cumulative Fish Biomass \( f(x) \)
$$f(x) = \frac{600}{1 + e^{-0.25(x - 12)}}$$

Where x is timeline in weeks, and f(x) is weight in grams per fish. Asymptote boundary limits size to 600g.

Instantaneous Growth Velocity \( f'(x) \)
$$f'(x) = \frac{150 \cdot e^{-0.25(x - 12)}}{\left(1 + e^{-0.25(x - 12)}\right)^2}$$

The derivative calculates growth speed in grams per week, identifying peak metabolism points.
